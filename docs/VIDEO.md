# The demo video — what to show, what to say

**Two cuts exist: a 75-second teaser (this shot list) and the 3:52 walkthrough that was submitted,
which keeps the loading screen, explains every round, the crowd, the village and the tech, with a
narrated voice track and captions in a letterbox band so nothing covers the gameplay.** The
first fifteen seconds decide whether a viewer watches the rest. Portrait, shot on a phone, because the whole claim of this project is that it was built
for one.

---

## Before you press record

1. **Two phones if at all possible.** One is you, one is a friend (or a second account on a
   tablet/laptop in the mobile client). The social half is the differentiator and it cannot be
   faked with one device. If you only have one, use the single-phone cut at the bottom.
2. **Screen-record on phone A** (Android: quick settings → Screen record, sound ON; iOS: Control
   Centre → Record). Phone B is the one you film *over the shoulder* for the two-players shot.
3. **Know the clock.** A round starts every 2 minutes on the dot. The lobby's NEXT UP board shows
   the next three rounds and the countdown. Stand in the lobby, read the board, and start
   recording about **25 seconds before** a round you want — that gives you the card, the 3-2-1
   and the whistle in one unbroken take.
4. **Which round to feature.** Crown Rush (two players fighting over the same ring) is the best
   shot in the game. Copycat is the most "only in Decentraland". Wait for the board to show one of
   them as NEXT UP before you start. Hex-Drop is always the fourth round of every show.
5. **Do a warm-up run first** so your hands know the route, then record. Record it **twice**.
6. Sound on. The crowd, the whistle and the announcer are half the atmosphere.

## The script

Say the words in **bold** out loud over the footage, or put them on screen as captions if you'd
rather not record voice. Either works; do not do both at length.

## If you only have one phone (60-second cut)

Drop the 0:40–0:48 pick/watch shot and the phone-B cutaway at 0:48. Replace the GG beat with the
rivalry line alone, and spend the saved seconds on the village walk. The claim changes from
"watch two people play" to "watch one person play a scene built for many" — still honest, still
strong. Everything else in the table is unchanged.

## Things not to do

- **Don't narrate the architecture.** No "the schedule is a pure function of UTC" in the video —
  that belongs in the README, and judges read it there. The video is for what it *feels* like.
- **Don't show a loading screen or a wait.** If you catch dead air between rounds, cut it.
- **Don't apologise for anything on camera.** If something looks rough, cut around it and reshoot.
- **Don't exceed 90 seconds.** Some hackathons disqualify over-length videos outright.
